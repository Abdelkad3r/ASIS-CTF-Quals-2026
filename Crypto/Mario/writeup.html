The leak in one picture

The AES key comes from a secret 24-dimensional subspace O hidden inside GF(16)96. Finding it from the public key is the intended hard problem. The generator also publishes 64 "reports" — and every one of them is a point of O pushed along the same direction g:

So the search collapses from find a 24-dimensional subspace of a 96-dimensional space to find a hyperplane of a 25-dimensional space, and that is linear algebra.

01

What the generator builds

Everything is over GF(16) with modulus x⁴+x+1, at n=96 variables, m=72 equations, oil dimension d=24.

oil_embed maps x to (K·x, x) for a secret 72×24 matrix K, so the oil space is O = {(K·x, x)}. build_public_map then fills each form with random coefficients and patches the oil-oil block so the form vanishes on O. The patch is a characteristic-2 trick worth spelling out:

poly[v+i][v+i] = eval_quad(poly, basis[i])

Because basis[i][v+i] = 1, adding coefficient c there shifts P(basis[i]) by exactly c. Choosing c = P(basis[i]) gives P + P = 0 in characteristic 2. The cross terms kill each P(basisᵢ + basisⱼ) the same way, which forces the polar form B(basisᵢ, basisⱼ) = 0. Zero on every basis vector and every pairwise sum means zero on the whole span.

The scramble is only a change of coordinates

monomial_scramble permutes and rescales the variables, and transform_poly applies the matching substitution so that P'(T(x)) = P(x). The published forms vanish on T(O) exactly as the originals vanished on O. The attack runs in public coordinates and never undoes it.

What is published

The key itself is HKDF over row_reduce(oil_basis). That is a full Gauss-Jordan reduction, so its output is the canonical RREF of the subspace — any basis of T(O) reproduces the same bytes. We need the subspace, not the generator's particular basis.

03

The leak

while True:
    g = r(n)
    if any(eval_quad(poly, g) for poly in polys):
        break                      # one g, drawn once

for _ in range(s):
    oil_vec = oil_embed(k_mat, r(d))
    mask = secrets.randbelow(15) + 1
    reports.append(vec_add(oil_vec, vec_scale(g, mask)))

Each report is rᵢ = oᵢ + λᵢ·g. The masks vary, but they only rescale the same vector, drawn once outside the loop. And g ∉ O, because it was chosen so that some form is nonzero at it while every form vanishes on O. So:

W = O ⊕ ⟨g⟩          dim W = 24 + 1 = 25

[+] span(reports) has dim 25  (expected 24 + 1)
04

Inside W, the forms factor

O is a hyperplane of W, so O = ker(l) for a linear form l that is unique up to scalar. Choose coordinates on W in which l is the last coordinate, so O = {c₂₅ = 0}. A quadratic form restricted to W vanishes on O, so every monomial not involving c₂₅ must have zero coefficient:

Q(c) = c₂₅ · ( Σ qᵢ,₂₅ cᵢ + q₂₅,₂₅ c₂₅ ) = l(c) · L(c)

Every one of the 72 forms, restricted to W, is a product of two linear forms — and they all share the factor l.

05

The polar form gives it away

B(u,v) = Q(u+v) + Q(u) + Q(v) = l(u)L(v) + l(v)L(u)

matrix:  l·Lᵀ + L·lᵀ      # symmetric, zero diagonal, rank exactly 2
kernel:  ker(l) ∩ ker(L)  ⊆  ker(l) = O    # 23 of the 24 dimensions

Each restricted polar form hands over a 23-dimensional slice of the 24-dimensional secret. Two different forms give two different slices, and inside a 24-dimensional space two distinct 23-dimensional subspaces already span it:

[+] poly 0: polar rank 2, kernel dim 23, oil span now 23
[+] poly 1: polar rank 2, kernel dim 23, oil span now 24

Computationally that is one matrix triple product per form: with W as a 25×96 matrix and S the symmetrised coefficient matrix, the restricted polar form is W·S·Wᵀ. In characteristic 2 the diagonal coefficients drop out of S entirely.

Verification and decryption

Lift the recovered coordinates back through the basis of W and evaluate every public form on every basis vector — 1728 evaluations, all zero. Then run the generator's own KDF over the canonical RREF.

07

What would have fixed it

One line out of place. g is drawn before the report loop and reused; draw it inside instead and each report becomes oᵢ + λᵢ·gᵢ with an independent direction. Those vectors span the whole of GF(16)⁹⁶ within a hundred samples and reveal nothing — there is no common 25-dimensional envelope left to intersect.

  • The scramble contributes nothing. It is a change of coordinates, and the attack is coordinate-free: it only asks which subspace the reports span and where the forms vanish. Obfuscation that commutes with the attack is not a defence.
  • The masks are a decoy. Random nonzero λᵢ look like they randomise each report, but scaling a fixed vector stays inside ⟨g⟩. Randomness that does not raise the dimension of what you publish hides nothing.
—

Takeaways

  • Count dimensions before doing algebra. The published data was 64 vectors in a 96-dimensional space. The only question worth asking first was how many dimensions they actually span; the answer, 25, made the rest routine.
  • A quadratic form vanishing on a hyperplane factors. Q = l·L is a small fact with a large consequence — the polar form drops to rank 2 and its kernel is a near-complete slice of the secret. Whenever a form is known to vanish on a codimension-1 subspace, take the polar form.
  • Auxiliary data is part of the attack surface. The public key was sound. The extra reports were what leaked, and a scheme is only as strong as everything shipped alongside it.
  • Verify with the AEAD tag. Recovering the right subspace produces a valid GCM tag, which is a proof rather than a plausibility argument.
